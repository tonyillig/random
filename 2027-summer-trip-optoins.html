Option 1 · Advisor's Pick

Japan

Tokyo · Kyoto · Osaka — 13 nights + 1 travel day
Bucket List ✓ Peak Dry Season High Season Pricing
$18–28k
All-in, party of 4
~$320–500/p/day
Why December
  • Cool and crisp — best photography conditions, Mt. Fuji views
  • Christmas illuminations rival Europe in scale and artistry
  • Dec 28–Jan 3 is Japan's "Oshogatsu" — cities actually empty out as locals go home; major sites become surprisingly quiet
  • Hatsumode (New Year's shrine visit, midnight Dec 31) is a once-in-a-lifetime cultural moment
  • No rain. Dry, clear days throughout.
Sample 13-Night Arc
  • Nights 1–2: Transit / Tokyo arrival, Shinjuku & Shibuya illuminations
  • Nights 3–5: Tokyo — Tsukiji, teamLab, Akihabara, Roppongi lights
  • Nights 6–8: Kyoto — temples, Arashiyama bamboo, kaiseki
  • Nights 9–10: Osaka — Dotonbori, street food, day trip to Nara
  • Nights 11–13: Back to Tokyo — Hakone hot springs & Mt. Fuji view, New Year's Eve at Meiji Shrine
Pros
  • Top bucket list item — checks Japan in a meaningful way
  • Every single day is actively interesting; pace fits Katelyn & Carson perfectly
  • Incredible food at every price point — especially relevant given lactose sensitivity (Japanese cuisine naturally dairy-light)
  • AA flies DFW → Tokyo Narita nonstop (JAL codeshare)
  • Dec 29–Jan 3 closures are actually a feature for tourists — shrines open, museums closed
  • Carson's Eagle Scout background will love the Shinto/Buddhist culture deep-dive
Cons
  • Hotel rates up 30–50% vs shoulder season — book now, 18 months out
  • Shinkansen (bullet train) seats sell out over New Year week — reserve in January 2027
  • Some restaurants close Dec 31–Jan 2; need backup plans
  • 13-hour flight is real — not Katelyn's or Kristin's first concern, but manageable
  • Yen strength variable — budget a buffer

Value tier (~$16k): economy flights, business hotels. Premium tier (~$22k): premium economy or upgraded hotels including a ryokan night. Splurge: business class pushes this to $26–28k.

⚑ Holiday Planning Flags
  • Shinkansen passes: Buy JR Passes from the US before departure — unavailable in Japan for foreigners once there. Book once you have confirmed dates.
  • Hatsumode (Dec 31–Jan 1): Meiji Shrine in Tokyo draws millions; go midnight–2am for the best experience. Plan accommodation near Harajuku.
  • Restaurant closures Dec 31–Jan 2: Many fine dining spots closed. Make 2–3 reservations 6–9 months out at top spots (Narisawa, etc.) — they fill for NYE very early.
  • teamLab Borderless: Reopened in Tokyo 2024. Pre-book timed entry — sells out weeks ahead in holiday season.
  • Universal Studios Osaka: If Carson wants it — Dec 24, 25, 31 waits hit 2–3 hrs. Weekday visit + Express Pass essential.

Value tier (~$12–13k): economy flights, solid mid-range hotels. Premium tier (~$17–19k): nicer beach resort (Anantara or similar), one or two luxury upgrades. At this price, Thailand punches well above its weight.

⚑ Holiday Planning Flags
  • Railay Beach (Krabi): No road access — reached only by longtail boat. Feels completely different from Phuket's resort-strip vibe. Consider 3–4 nights here over New Year's instead of Phuket proper.
  • Gala dinner surcharges: Major resorts add a $75–150/person mandatory NYE dinner. Factor into hotel comparisons — it's often not disclosed in the room rate.
  • Phi Phi Islands day trip: Buy through your hotel — do NOT go Dec 31 or Jan 1, boats are dangerously overcrowded.
  • Elephant sanctuary Chiang Mai: Ethical operators (Elephant Nature Park) book out weeks in advance during holiday week. Reserve now.
  • Book beach hotel by Sep 2026: Beachfront in Krabi/Phuket over Christmas/NYE sells out. Shoulder-season pricing will be gone — just focus on securing your preferred property.

  • English-speaking, easy logistics — lowest "friction" trip on this list

Entry point of ~$21k is realistic if you book 10–12 months ahead and stay mid-range. Premium execution (waterfront hotel, liveaboard reef, proper NYE venue) runs $28–33k. Business class would add another $10k+.

⚑ Holiday Planning Flags
  • Sydney NYE viewing venues: Circular Quay, Bennelong Point, and McMahons Point waterfront restaurants book NYE packages 6–9 months out. Some require a minimum spend of A$400–800/person. Book no later than March 2027.
  • Airfare timing: Book DFW–SYD 10–12 months ahead; holiday fares double vs. shoulder season. This route often has fewer options than Tokyo or Bangkok.
  • Great Barrier Reef bleaching advisory: Mass bleaching events have occurred in recent years due to warming. Outer reef sites generally hold better coral health than inshore. Use Cairns Dive Centre or Quicksilver Connections liveaboard for best outer reef access — book 4–6 months out.
  • Australian ETA visa: ~A$20/person, applied via the Australian ETA app — takes minutes. Required for US citizens before departure.

Advisor's Read
Bottom Line

Japan is the pick. Thailand is the smart play if the budget needs to land under $16k.

Japan wins this cleanly. It's your top bucket-list destination, it's genuinely spectacular in December (the holiday illuminations alone are worth it), and the cultural immersion is exactly what your family's travel style rewards. Carson's Eagle Scout mind will eat up the Shinto shrines and the social discipline. Katelyn will thrive with the pace — active sightseeing, new things daily, no need to baby the experience. Kristin gets world-class food that's naturally easy on dairy. And you and Kristin get a trip that actually justifies "bucket list." The New Year's Eve at Meiji Shrine is one of those rare shared-experience moments that families talk about for the rest of their lives. Japan in December is not Japan at its cheapest, but it's Japan at its most magical.

Thailand is the smart play if you want to land closer to $13–16k all-in, or if the 13-hour flight is a deal-breaker. The Krabi/Railay Beach + Bangkok + Chiang Mai circuit is outstanding — it's just a different kind of trip (more beach-and-explore, less cultural punch). Nothing wrong with that, and the value is genuinely hard to beat.

Australia is expensive for what it is in late December — you're paying a big premium for peak season and the NYE positioning. Sydney NYE is legitimately world-class and the Great Barrier Reef is irreplaceable, but at $28–33k for a premium execution you're paying Rhine Cruise money for a trip that sits below it on cultural richness. I'd save Australia for a shoulder-season trip (March/April or October/November) where you get everything for $18–22k instead.

One honest caution on Japan: the Dec 28–Jan 3 window is Japan's busiest domestic travel period. Hotels and Shinkansen fill up. If you're going, decisions need to happen by fall 2026 at the latest — ideally, you're booking airfare and key hotels in the next 3–4 months.
